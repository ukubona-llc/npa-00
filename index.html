01

The problem, in plain numbers

Uganda's population report is clear: mental illness is no longer a marginal health issue. It is a direct threat to the Ten-Fold Growth Strategy. A country aiming for USD 500 billion by 2040 cannot afford to lose the productivity, education, and labour capacity of its largest-ever youth cohort to an untreated, underreported, and underfunded condition.

Prevalence
24.2%
of adults live with a mental health condition. Depression affects 21.2%, anxiety 20.2%. Most have never received care.
Children & youth
22.9%
of children under 18 affected. In Kampala, 66% of adolescents report poor mental well-being. These are the workers of 2040.
Reported cases
494K → 843K
Facility-reported cases rose 71% from 2021 to 2024. These are only the visible cases — most go unreported entirely.
The treatment gap
Fewer than 1 in 10 people in need receive care
Limited infrastructure, workforce shortages, stigma, and financing constraints combine to make Uganda one of the most underserved mental health environments in the region.
Demographic context
The emergency is inside the demographic dividend
Over 73% of Ugandans are under 30. More than half of young people aged 18–30 are not in education, employment, or training. Mental ill-health is accelerating that exclusion.

Scaled-up prevention, early identification, and community-based care is measurably cheaper than the projected productivity losses. This is a finance argument, not just a health argument.

04

The workforce gap

The system cannot respond to demand it cannot staff. The gap is not at the specialist level — it is at the community-facing level where early detection and continuity of care must happen.

Posts required
9,661 posts
Posts filled
4,290 filled
Gap
5,371 missing
55% gap
Where the gap lives
Over 96% of required posts are at Health Centre III, HC IV, and General Hospital level — not at national referral hospitals. The problem is not specialist scarcity at the top. It is the absence of community-facing cadres at the bottom, where early detection, follow-up, and continuity of care should occur.

Tensor — SUPRE 2025 as the uncollapsed state
The population report holds every relevant variable at once: prevalence by age and region, workforce gaps by facility level, economic costs by scenario, community engagement needs, legal and governance constraints. Before any intervention is decided, this full picture must be held — not simplified prematurely.
Variables: Prevalence (adults 24.2%, children 22.9%) · DALYs · Facility cases (843,295) · Workforce gaps (5,300+) · Urban stress patterns · Conflict region trauma · Refugee burden · Urbanisation pressure · Financing constraints
Compressor — the district digital twin applies weights
Ukubona's digital twin takes the full tensor and applies weights by district: how much does workforce gap matter here vs. access distance vs. prevalence vs. economic cost? The ROYGBIV heat map is the visual output of this compression. The policy maker selects their priority — workforce, access, cost, prevalence — and the twin reweights in real time.
Longitude × Latitude: 135 districts · Altitude: ROYGBIV burden intensity · Drop-down: policymaker's priority choice · Each selection produces a different vector.
Vector — the direction of action the compression produces
Once the district twin has applied weights, it does not produce a final answer — it produces a direction. In Gulu: prioritise PTSD-trained community workers. In Kampala: target adolescent services and substance use. In rural HC IIIs: address the 96% of the staffing gap that lives at community level. The vector is specific to geography and priority.
Community engagement phases: Before — district sensitisation and consent · During — facility-linked referral and diagnosis · After — outcome tracking and system update
Invariant — what the data says regardless of which variables shift
Some findings hold no matter which scenario is run. The eigen___ in this context: productivity loss always exceeds treatment cost. Workforce gaps at community level always dominate specialist gaps. Early detection always yields higher return than late-stage treatment. These invariants survive every compression — they are the spine of the planning argument.
The framework shapes interpretation of findings: a district-level pattern is only treated as a planning signal if it survives the ethical and economic compression. Noise at the tensor level does not become policy.
Scalar — the earned decision, not the assumed one
The final output is a district-specific, priority-weighted intervention recommendation. It is earned by running the full pipeline — not assumed from national averages. If the outcome is inequitable or ineffective, the error is upstream: the tensor was incomplete, or the compressor weights were wrong. This is what makes the system updatable.
Pipeline: District → NPA → Ukubona → Intervention → Update · The feedback loop closes when outcomes are measured and the twin is recalibrated. Justice is the result of a process, not an assumption.
